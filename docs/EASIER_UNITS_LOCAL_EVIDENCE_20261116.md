# Easier units for stock and recipes — local evidence

Recorded: 2026-11-16. Branch: `workstream-c/easier-units`, based on `main` at
`6499cde`. The owner found entering stock and recipe amounts fiddly. For
example, oat milk comes in a carton but a latte uses 10 fl oz, and a Biscoff
spread jar is bought by the jar but used by the scoop. The owner tried a local
preview and approved all five changes on 2026-11-16. **Local evidence only.**
This change adds migration `0050`; no other schema-bearing change is pending.

## Changed behavior

1. **Setup as one sentence**
   ([`stock-entry.tsx`](../src/components/workspace/stock-entry.tsx)):
   - The form reads "Heavy cream comes in a [carton] that holds [32] [fl oz]".
     Units are grouped as Liquid, Weight, Items and Other units.
   - The recipe unit follows the container's unit and can be changed in the
     line "In recipes, measure it in".
   - A starting guess from the product's name fills in the container and unit,
     for example milk → carton and fl oz, syrup → bottle, beans → bag, cups →
     sleeve. Amounts are never guessed.
   - Existing products show their saved setup. "Change how it comes or is
     measured" keeps the stock total and history, as before.
2. **Everyday units for the café**
   ([`unit-preferences.ts`](../src/lib/unit-preferences.ts)):
   - Owners and managers choose **US (fl oz, oz, lb)** or **Metric (mL, g,
     kg)** above the stock list. The default is US.
   - The choice is stored per company in `company_preferences` (migration
     `0050`), with who changed it and when.
   - It changes only display and menus: menus list everyday units first, and
     the stock list shows "3½ cartons · 112 fl oz". The purchasing and
     count-history views show everyday units instead of raw mL or g.
3. **Recipes**
   ([`recipe-amount-fields.tsx`](../src/components/workspace/recipe-amount-fields.tsx)):
   - Choosing an ingredient selects the product's own unit. The menu lists
     everyday units, then "Other units".
   - A hint relates the amount to what is bought, such as "about 6 per carton".
   - Recipe lists and reviews use short unit names, and a product's own
     measure shows by name ("pumps") instead of an internal ID.
   - Editing an item or modifier keeps amounts as entered (10 fl oz) instead
     of converting them to mL
     ([`modifier-editor.ts`](../src/lib/modifier-editor.ts)).
4. **Counting:**
   - Opening and physical counts take full containers, then "An open carton:
     None, ¼, ½, ¾ left, or an exact amount" in everyday units.
   - Items counted singly take loose items instead.
   - The share is sent through the existing package quantity, as a remainder
     in g or mL, so no server change was needed.
5. **Pumps and scoops:**
   - "1 pump = 0.25 fl oz" becomes the product's own measure, an exact reduced
     fraction of mL, g or items. This replaces the "advanced" numerator and
     denominator fields.
   - Setup shows "One bottle holds 25.4 fl oz, about 102 pumps". Recipes can
     use "2 pumps" or still use fl oz.

The API gains `setMeasurementSystemExact` on `/api/inventory` (exact inventory
preview), for owners and managers, and `GET` includes `measurementSystem`.

## Schema review

- **SQL, snapshot and journal:** generated by `pnpm db:generate` from
  [`schema.ts`](../src/db/schema.ts): one table keyed by company, with a check
  allowing only `us` or `metric`.
- **Fresh and populated databases:** `pnpm db:check` applied all 51
  migrations to fresh SQLite. `pnpm db:migrate:local` applied `0050` to the
  existing local development database.
- **Compatibility:** the change is additive. A company without a row reads as
  US. Code from before this change ignores the table.
- **Rollback / forward repair:** revert the code; the table can stay. Stored
  stock, recipes and history are unaffected either way.

## Verification

The complete local pipeline passed in this worktree on 2026-11-16 with the
changes staged: `pnpm typecheck`, `pnpm test` (180 suites), `pnpm db:check`
(51 migrations), `pnpm build`, `pnpm db:migrate:local` and `pnpm test:local`.

- [`tests/unit-preferences.mjs`](../tests/unit-preferences.mjs) covers:
  - everyday units and short labels, and switching to lb or L for large
    amounts;
  - container wording and plurals ("3½ cartons", "less than ¼ carton");
  - per-container hints;
  - open-container shares;
  - exact pump factors and refusals of bad amounts;
  - name guesses;
  - the setting's default, per-company isolation, refusals, and the database
    check;
  - an end-to-end run on the real stores:
    - a syrup set up with 1 pump = 0.25 fl oz and opened as "2 full bottles,
      one ½ left" reads 2½ bottles (63.5 fl oz);
    - oat milk opened with an exact 20 fl oz remainder;
    - a latte sale using 10 fl oz of oat milk and 2 pumps uses 10 fl oz and
      0.5 fl oz ("about 50 per bottle");
    - editing keeps 2 fl oz as entered.
- [`tests/m2-security.mjs`](../tests/m2-security.mjs), for the new action:
  - it joins the per-action cross-company and cross-site checks;
  - anonymous (401), other-company, employee and wrong-company manager
    requests (403) are refused, and "imperial" returns 400;
  - employees read US by default, then metric after a manager changes it;
  - only company A's row changes, recording who changed it.
- [`tests/inventory-management-contract.mjs`](../tests/inventory-management-contract.mjs):
  the empty-company reads now include the default `measurementSystem`.
- **Mutations:** eight deliberate code mutations were tried, and seven made a
  test fail:
  - wrong open-container share;
  - wrong pump factor;
  - wrong hint;
  - never switching to lb or L;
  - ignoring the stored setting;
  - the service accepting any value;
  - editing converting to mL.

  The eighth loosened the API's own check, and the service still refused the
  value, so the request was still refused.
- **Local browser checks** against `pnpm dev` with fixture sign-in, with no
  page errors:
  - heavy cream was guessed as carton and fl oz; 32 fl oz with 3 full and one
    ½ left saved as "3½ cartons · 112 fl oz";
  - vanilla syrup with 1 pump = 0.25 fl oz showed about 102 pumps per bottle;
  - an oat milk count of 2 full and one ¾ left saved as 2¾ cartons;
  - in recipes, oat milk at 10 fl oz showed a per-carton hint, and vanilla
    defaulted to pumps with "about 50 per bottle";
  - switching to metric showed "3.31 L" and switching back showed "112 fl oz";
  - at 390 px wide there was no sideways scrolling.
